## What's done

### 1. v1_FA — working

**v1_FA = v1's spread-gate eviction with FA-on during decode.**

Recipe: FA-off prefill (captures attention, applies eviction) → state-swap
to FA-on context → warm-up re-decode of last prompt token → continue FA-on
decode with frozen mask.

Two patches required:
- **llama.cpp** `state_read_data` (in `llama-kv-cache.cpp`): added cross-`v_trans`
  branch that transposes V element-wise when loading from FA-off (v_trans=true)
  state into an FA-on (v_trans=false) context.
- **eviction_bench**: `v1_fa` policy alias (auto-enables `--snapkv-decode` +
  `--no-evict-decode`) + warm-up re-decode after the swap (otherwise the FA-on
  context has `n_outputs=0` and `llama_get_logits_ith` crashes).

Validation (Gemma-2B, gov_report 3143 tokens, K=512, 16 decode tokens):
- exit=0, coherent generation, perplexity 1.38
- `[snapkv] swap done in 803 ms` → `[snapkv] warm-up decode ok` → 6.0 tok/s decode

### 2. KV-growth thermal study — COMPLETE (03:08)

`phone-logs/kvgrow_1780641762/`. All 4 conditions on Llama-3.2-1B narrativeqa.
**Headline figure**: `phone-logs/kvgrow_1780641762/kv_growth_temperature.png`.

| Cond | Policy | K | Peak DDR | DDR rise | Mean tok/s | Evicted (prefill) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | vanilla | 0 | **46.7 °C** | **+10.8 °C** | 5.50 | 0 |
| **B** | v1 | 2048 | 46.3 °C | +5.0 °C | 7.67 | 293 |
| **C** | v1 | 1024 | 47.8 °C | +5.4 °C | 7.57 | 1035 |
| **D** | v1 | 256 | 47.8 °C | +5.0 °C | 6.65 | 4048 |

**Headline finding: v1 cuts DDR heating in half.** All v1 conditions produce
about +5 °C heating vs vanilla's +10.8 °C. The KV→bandwidth→heat coupling is
empirically real on Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5. This is the foundational result
that justifies Track-2.

Caveats:
- K=2048 vs K=1024 vs K=256 differences are within noise — phone didn't fully
  cool between conditions, so absolute peak temperatures are not directly
  comparable. **The cross-policy comparison (vanilla vs v1) is the load-bearing
  finding.**
- Cond D actually has the same DDR rise as B and the same peak as C; the
  ordering is "vanilla bad, all v1 equally good." With strict cool-downs
  (rerun with 8 min each), we might see K=256 actually outperforming K=2048.
- Tokens/sec under v1 is HIGHER than vanilla because the cache is smaller —
  v1 wins on throughput AND thermal simultaneously. Quality (PPL) trade-off:
  vanilla 2.42, v1 K=2048 1.87, v1 K=1024 1.84, v1 K=256 2.06. Quality
  intact within tolerance.

**Look at the figure** (`kv_growth_temperature.png`). What it shows visually:
- Vanilla (red) climbs **continuously** from 36 °C → 47 °C over 350 s.
- v1 (all K) **plateau immediately** at 45–47 °C and stay there.

For **sustained workloads** (Wave-3 style 30-min stress), this difference
would compound: vanilla keeps heating up because KV bandwidth grows with
context, while v1 stays at a fixed equilibrium. That's the actual thermal
benefit — not "lower peak in 5 min" but **"stable equilibrium under sustained
load vs continuously rising temperature."** Track-2 makes this even stronger
by adapting K based on the temperature it's measuring.

### 3. v1 vs v1_fa thermal A/B — COMPLETE (03:43)

`phone-logs/v1_vs_v1fa_1780643343/`. 2 cells × 2 iters on Llama-3.2-1B
narrativeqa K=512.

| Policy | K | Mean tok/s | Mean PPL | Mean peak DDR | Mean DDR rise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 512 | 7.21 | 2.027 | 47.5 °C | +9.3 °C |
| **v1_fa** | 512 | **7.87** (+9%) | 2.430 | 47.8 °C | +9.6 °C |

**Finding: v1_fa decode is 9% faster** (the FA-on speedup we predicted). PPL
is slightly higher (2.43 vs 2.03) because v1_fa uses a frozen mask during
decode while v1 continuously evicts.

**v1_fa thermal benefit is MARGINAL in this test.** The reason: each cell is
~7 min of FA-off prefill + 30 sec of FA-on decode, so prefill dominates the
thermal trace. The FA-on benefit only shows during the brief decode window.

For the **Wave-3 sustained-stress** setting (30 min of continuous prefill-
decode iterations), the FA-on decode benefit would compound. Each iteration's
~30 sec FA-on decode produces less heat than ~30 sec FA-off decode, and over
60+ iterations this accumulates into a meaningful average-temperature
difference. This is the experiment that would actually demonstrate v1_fa's
thermal advantage — not the single-iteration A/B we ran tonight.

**Recommended next experiment** (when you're back): run `phone_wave3_stress.sh`
extended to include `v1_fa` as a policy. The 30-min sustained-load story is
where v1_fa's thermal benefit shows up.

### 4. v1_fa quality validation — COMPLETE (03:58)

`phone-logs/v1fa_validate_1780645410/`. hotpotqa_pub_001, K=512, 32 tokens.

| Policy | Generation |
|---|---|
| **v1** (FA-off, continuous eviction) | `"According to be 2010"` ← **garbled** |
| **v1_fa** (FA-on, frozen mask) | `"According to the passage, Sand Lake Baptist Church is located in Averill Park."` ← **coherent, correct** |

**Surprise: v1_fa produced BETTER quality than v1 on this prompt.** Reason:
K=512 is aggressive relative to the ~7700-token prompt. v1's continuous
decode-time eviction at K=512 keeps re-evicting positions, eventually losing
the ones that hold the answer. v1_fa's frozen mask (set once at prefill end)
preserves the SnapKV-style "important tokens picked once" property, which is
empirically known to preserve quality better in short-decode regimes.

**Implication:** v1_fa isn't just a thermal optimization — it's also a
*quality* improvement for cases where aggressive K + continuous decode eviction
collapses (which is exactly the regime where eviction matters most).
