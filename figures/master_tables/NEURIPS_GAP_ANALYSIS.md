## 2. What Each Theorem Needs, Concretely

### Theorem 1 — Cumulative T-step Attention Regret Bound (headline candidate)

**Statement status:** Publishable in principle. Closes a gap KeepKV explicitly leaves open and extends Ada-KV's per-step bound to the cumulative case, which is the regime that actually matters for long-decode workloads.

**Proof status:** Drafted but did NOT survive adversarial verification.

**Likely failure modes (must be diagnosed before fixing):**
1. **Coupling between S_t and a_{t,i}.** The retained set S_t at step t is chosen using softmax weights that depend on the cache history, which itself depends on previously evicted tokens. The drafted proof almost certainly treated m_t as exogenous; it is not. We need a martingale or adaptive-adversary argument (likely an Azuma–Hoeffding variant on the filtration generated by past evictions).
2. **The sqrt(T) * sqrt(sum m_t^2) form is suspicious.** Cauchy–Schwarz gives sum m_t <= sqrt(T) * sqrt(sum m_t^2), so the bound collapses to sum_t m_t if m_t is summable. The interesting regime (gamma in (1/2, 1)) needs a sharper concentration argument than what was drafted.
3. **Assumption A3 (power-law tail decay of retained mass) is empirical, not axiomatic.** We must either (a) prove it follows from a reasonable generative assumption on attention, or (b) measure it on our OnePlus 15 traces and report the fitted gamma with confidence intervals. Option (b) makes the theorem conditional, which is fine, but the conditioning must be explicit.

**Estimated time to a verified proof:** 4–8 weeks of focused theory work, assuming we have a co-author or advisor comfortable with online-learning / adversarial-bandit regret proofs. Without that expertise, 3–4 months.

### Theorem 4 — Heuristic-vs-Ada-KV Optimum under Power-Law Sparsity (supporting appendix)

**Statement status:** Publishable as an appendix theorem. Justifies our O(H) closed-form allocator vs Ada-KV's Algorithm 1.

**Proof status:** Also did not survive adversarial verification.

**Likely failure modes:**
1. The power-law sparsity assumption must be tied to a measurable quantity on real traces.
2. The "gap to optimum" claim needs to be a worst-case bound, not an average-case one, or reviewers will object that the average case is not informative for a safety-critical eviction policy.

**Estimated time:** 2–4 weeks once Theorem 1's proof framework is settled (the techniques transfer).

### Theorems 2 and 3

Already demoted by the audit. Theorem 2 is a corollary of FlashAttention Theorem 1; Theorem 3 is incremental over "More Tokens, Lower Precision" (EMNLP-F 2025) and "Don't Waste Bits!" (2026). Neither belongs in a NeurIPS submission as a numbered theorem.

3. **Power-law fit for retained-mass decay (gamma estimation).** Required to instantiate Theorem 1's rate. **Time: 1 week given (1) is done.**

## 7. Second-Round Adversarial Verification — Update 2026-06-12

After the initial gap analysis above, we iterated Theorem 1's proof through **two further adversarial-review passes** (4 reviewers in pass 1, 3 reviewers in pass 2) with substantive rewrites between rounds. The proof did not pass the survival threshold.

### 7.1 Threshold and Outcome

- **Threshold for "survived":** at least 2 of 3 reviewers in pass 2 returning `proof_correct = true`.
- **Actual pass-2 vote:** 1 of 3 correct.
- **Verdict:** **Theorem 1 has NOT survived.** It must remain demoted to an informal motivation lemma in the systems track and cannot serve as the headline contribution of an ICLR/NeurIPS submission as currently written.

### 7.2 Pass 1 Failure Modes (4 reviewers, all `correct=false`)

Aggregated and de-duplicated across the four pass-1 reviews:

1. **A4 (the central tail-decay assumption) carries the entire proof but is not derived for the practical fixed-eviction-budget B setting.** Reviewers consistently flagged that A4 is *assumed* rather than reduced to something measurable or to a published concentration result.
2. **Vacuous Azuma term for deterministic decoding.** For greedy decoding the martingale increments are degenerate, yet the Azuma–Hoeffding term remains in the final bound. This makes the stated rate provably looser than the trivial bound `sum_t m_t <= T * m_max` for moderate horizons T — i.e. the headline rate is dominated by the trivial bound in exactly the regime we claim to improve.
3. **Empirical calibration of A4 is unverifiable and partially contradicted by published evidence.** Reviewers cited measured attention-sink concentration (Xiao et al. StreamingLLM) and pyramidal-attention patterns (PyramidKV, PyramidInfer) as evidence that the retained-mass tail is not the smooth power law A4 assumes for the heads/layers we claim to cover.
4. **A concrete StreamingLLM-style counterexample falsifies A4 on attention-sink heads.** For heads dominated by a small set of fixed positional sinks, the post-eviction tail of the retained mass does not decay in the way A4 requires; the proof's coverage claim ("for all standard transformer attention heads") is therefore false.
5. **Gap between headline rate and what is actually derived.** Two reviewers independently noted that even granting A1–A5, what the proof establishes is a deterministic envelope under a recency-reserve hypothesis, not the advertised `O(T^gamma * polylog)` martingale rate.

### 7.3 Pass 2 Failure Modes (3 reviewers, 1 correct / 2 not correct)

After rewriting to address pass-1 points (adding hybrid sink+recency reserve to the policy, weakening A4 to A4-POS, separating deterministic and stochastic decoding cases), pass 2 produced:

1. **Reviewer A (correct=false, score 4 — Borderline reject).** Substantial framework, but **the martingale layer is largely vacuous given the deterministic pathwise bound that the rewrite added.** The headline rate, once the martingale term is removed (since it is dominated), is just integration of a deterministic envelope under a *restrictive recency-reserve hypothesis that is not formally listed among A1–A5*. In other words, the rewrite moved a load-bearing assumption out of the assumption list and into the body of the proof, which reviewers correctly flagged.
2. **Reviewer B (correct=false, score 5 — Borderline accept).** Substantially improved over v2; main bound is essentially correct under hybrid sink+recency policies, but **A4-POS as written is internally inconsistent with the StreamingLLM-motivated sink phenomenon** (the very phenomenon the hybrid policy was added to handle). Several minor algebra / statement issues also remain (constants in the recency-reserve term, off-by-one in the cumulative sum, undefined symbols in the appendix lemma).
3. **Reviewer C (correct=true, score 5 — borderline reject to weak accept; NeurIPS workshop fit, marginal main-conference).** The single accept-leaning vote treats the result as a workshop-quality contribution rather than a main-track theorem.

### 7.4 Root-Cause Pattern Across Both Rounds

The same structural problem reappeared in both passes, just relocated:

- **Pass 1:** the load-bearing assumption is A4 (smooth power-law tail), which is empirically falsified for sink heads.
- **Pass 2:** the load-bearing assumption is the *recency-reserve hypothesis*, which was moved into the body of the proof and is also internally inconsistent with the sink-head regime it is supposed to cover (A4-POS).

In both cases, the proof tries to prove `O(T^gamma * polylog)` for *all* standard transformer heads, but the only regime where the techniques actually go through is *non-sink, non-pyramidal, smoothly-decaying* heads. That is not a sufficient coverage class for the headline claim.

### 7.5 What Would Need to Change

1. **Narrow the coverage class explicitly.** State Theorem 1 as a bound for heads satisfying a *measured* tail-decay condition (gamma estimated per-head from real traces), not for "all standard attention heads." This makes the theorem conditional and verifiable, but loses the headline universality.
2. **Drop the martingale layer in the deterministic-decoding statement.** Two separate theorems: a deterministic pathwise bound for greedy decoding (no Azuma term) and a martingale bound for sampling decoders (with a properly non-degenerate increment).
3. **Promote the recency-reserve hypothesis to an explicit assumption A6**, and state under what conditions on the eviction policy it holds (e.g. StreamingLLM-style fixed sink + sliding window).
4. **Replace A4 with a measurable per-head condition** plus an empirical section that fits gamma on real OnePlus 15 traces and reports head-level histograms of gamma with confidence intervals, explicitly flagging the heads where the assumption fails and what fraction of total attention mass they carry.
5. **Re-derive Theorem 4 (heuristic-vs-Ada-KV gap) as a worst-case bound** on the narrowed coverage class, rather than the current power-law-sparsity average-case statement.

### 7.6 Updated Time-to-Submission Estimate

The pass-1/pass-2 cycle cost roughly the budget that the original table allocated to *one* re-derivation pass. We have used that budget and the result is not yet at survival threshold. A realistic re-estimate:

## 8. Round 3 — Lower-Bound Attack on Cumulative Eviction Regret (FAILED, 2026-06-12)

### 8.1 Pivot Rationale

After the upper-bound Theorem 1 failed two adversarial verification rounds (Sections 6 and 7), we pivoted from "upper bound on Ada-KV regret" to the dual theoretical question: **a lower bound on the cumulative \(\ell_2\) attention-reconstruction regret achievable by any causal eviction policy with budget \(B\)**. A lower bound is structurally easier than an upper bound because it only requires constructing one adversarial sequence rather than reasoning about all sequences. The draft was prepared at `/home/mislam22/EndurKV_workspace/EndurKV/docs/theory/cumulative_eviction_regret_lower_bound.tex` (330 lines), claiming \(\Omega(\sigma T)\) cumulative regret in the linear-budget regime via a planted-block + Rademacher-value construction reduced through Yao's minimax principle, vector Khintchine, and Fano subset-selection.

### 8.2 Round-3 Verdicts

Three independent adversarial reviewers were tasked with stress-testing the lower-bound draft:

1. **Reviewer A (correct=false): REFUTED.** Identified a fatal arithmetic error in the per-block Khintchine application (Sec 5, eq. 14/15): a \(\sqrt{k^\star}\) factor is silently dropped between the Khintchine \(s\)-coefficient and the boxed per-block bound. After correction, the headline \(\Omega(\sigma T)\) collapses to at most \(\Omega(\sigma T / B^{3/2})\) in the linear regime — exponentially weaker than claimed. A direct counterexample (the empty-set policy \(T_m'=\emptyset\)) gives residual \(\Theta(\sigma/\sqrt{k^\star})\) by CLT, contradicting eq. (13)'s claim of \(\ge c_0\sigma(1-B/k^\star)\) for any \(T_m'\). The summation step in Sec 7 then masks the error with an invalid "absorb \(1/k^\star\) into the constant" move; \(k^\star\) is a function of \(T\) and \(B\), not a constant. Lemma 3 (planted-subset Fano) is vestigial because the construction is i.i.d. given the block direction \(e_m\); the planted subset \(S_m^\star\) is never used. The non-terminal-loss bound (eq. 11) is asserted without proof and is likely \(\Theta(\sigma\sqrt{T})\) by Khintchine on the per-step residual, dominating the corrected terminal contribution.

2. **Reviewer B (correct=false): REFUTED IN PART (with framing-level category errors).** The bound is non-trivial in absolute magnitude (\(\Omega(\sigma T) > \Omega(1)\)) but is actually a **linear-regret** statement, a constant fraction of the maximum achievable loss \(2\sigma T\) — not a sublinear regret in the Cesa-Bianchi–Lugosi tradition. Calling it "regret" is a category error: classical online-learning lower bounds (expert advice \(\Omega(\sqrt{T \log N})\), OCO \(\Omega(\sqrt{T})\), online caching \(\Omega(\sqrt{T})\), Bhattacharjee–Sinha) are sublinear and compete against a fixed-in-hindsight comparator; this quantity is \(\ell_2\) approximation error to the unrestricted full-context oracle, a different object. The construction also smuggles in hidden restrictions: (a) adversary tailored to architecture (\(R\) scales with \(\sqrt{d \log k^\star}/\tau\)); (b) Assumption 1 lets \(r = O(\sqrt{\log T})\) grow with horizon, masking that the bound *needs* \(r\) to grow for softmax sharpening — under the natural \(r=O(1)\) the \(o_T(1)\) residual is not small; (c) \(d\) must implicitly grow as \(\Omega(\log T)\); (d) planted subset has size exactly \(B+1\) (knife-edge); (e) non-terminal residual claim is unproven and cross-block tails look \(\Theta(\sigma)\); (f) Proposition 2's "matching upper bound" is the trivial \(\ell_t \le 2\sigma\) ceiling — both bounds coincide at the maximum-loss ceiling, which is not informative tightness; (g) Lemma 3 captures miss-count not missed-mass, the reduction is only sketched for the worst case.

3. **Reviewer C (correct=true, BORDERLINE LEAN-ACCEPT, conditional on five hard blockers).** Estimates the current draft at ~25–30% NeurIPS acceptance probability, rising to ~60% after fixing the five hard blockers and ~75% with a benign-distribution corollary plus one empirical figure. Hard blockers identified: (1) Lemma 3 binomial statement is vacuous as written (\(\binom{B}{B+1}=0\) makes the inequality empty); (2) non-terminal residual \(O(\sigma/\sqrt{k^\star})\) is asserted without derivation; (3) cross-block \(o_T(1)\) residual needs an actual sub-Gaussian tail bound; (4) Lemma 2 vector-Khintchine uses sloppy \(\|x\|_2 \ge \|x\|_1/\sqrt{d}\) — should cite Latala 1997 or Marcus–Pisier for the true isotropic constant; (5) bibliography has only four placeholder entries. Soft blockers: missing benign-distribution corollary, Gaussian noise should be truncated to bounded sub-Gaussian, spread-bounded class needs formal definition via attention entropy, all `[TIGHTEN]` markers must be stripped from the submitted version. **The "accept" is conditional on a revision cycle that fixes structural mathematical defects, not a clean accept.**

### 8.3 Survivor Count

**0 of 3 reviewers issued a clean accept.** Reviewers A and B independently identified concrete mathematical defects — the missing \(\sqrt{k^\star}\) factor in the Khintchine application, the unproven non-terminal residual claim, the trivial "tightness" against \(2\sigma T\), and the category error of calling \(\ell_2\) approximation error "regret." Reviewer C lean-accepts only conditional on a multi-week revision that itself addresses the same structural issues raised by Reviewers A and B. Under the **\(\ge 2/3\) clean-accept** threshold specified by the verification protocol, the lower-bound proof FAILS.

### 8.4 Convergent Defects Across Reviewers A, B, and C

The three independent reviews independently flagged the same load-bearing defects, which gives high confidence that the defects are real rather than reviewer noise:

1. **Non-terminal loss bound (eq. nonterm-loss) is asserted, not derived.** Flagged by all three reviewers as a structural gap. If the cross-block tail contributes \(\Theta(\sigma)\) per step (as Reviewer A's Khintchine calculation suggests), the cumulative non-terminal contribution is \(\Theta(\sigma\sqrt{T})\) or worse, which either dominates or wipes out the (corrected) terminal contribution.

2. **Lemma 3 (planted-subset Fano) is broken or vestigial.** Reviewer A: "vestigial — the construction never USES \(S_m^\star\)." Reviewer B: "captures miss-count not missed-mass, reduction only sketched." Reviewer C: "binomial statement is vacuous as written, rewrite as direct cardinality argument."

3. **Tightness claim is trivial.** Reviewer A: keep-last-\(B\) achieves \(O(\sigma T / \sqrt{k^\star})\) on the construction, far below the claimed \(\Theta(\sigma T)\). Reviewer B: both bounds coincide at the maximum-loss ceiling \(2\sigma T\). Reviewer C: "matches only the TRIVIAL upper bound, not a competitive baseline."

4. **Assumption 1's \(r = O(\sqrt{\log T})\) is a hidden horizon-coupling.** Reviewers A and B both note that the softmax-sharpening machinery requires \(R = \Theta(\sqrt{d \log k^\star})\) growing with horizon; under the natural \(r=O(1)\) the construction's residual is not exponentially small.

### 8.5 Root-Cause Pattern Across All Three Rounds

| Round | Direction | Load-bearing defect | Outcome |
|-------|-----------|--------------------|---------|
| 1 | Upper bound on Ada-KV regret (Theorem 1, A4 smooth power-law tail) | A4 empirically falsified for sink heads | REFUTED |
| 2 | Upper bound on Ada-KV regret (Theorem 1 v2, recency-reserve hypothesis) | Recency-reserve incompatible with sink/pyramidal regimes A4-POS | REFUTED |
| 3 | Lower bound on cumulative eviction regret (planted-block construction) | (a) Khintchine misapplied; (b) non-terminal residual unproven; (c) trivial tightness; (d) "regret" framing is a category error | REFUTED |

The pattern is consistent: each attempt makes a clean technical step (power-law tails; recency reserve; planted-block + Khintchine) but the load-bearing assumption either (i) does not hold in the regime the headline theorem claims to cover, or (ii) is silently weakened in the calculation to make the rate look better than it is. In all three rounds the construction or proof apparatus is *partly* correct; the failure is at the headline-rate level, not at the basic-machinery level.

### 8.6 Honest Conclusion

We have now explored the two main theoretical directions for the EndurKV/Ada-KV theory section:

- **Upper bound** (\(O(T^\gamma \cdot \text{polylog})\) on Ada-KV regret): failed in Rounds 1 and 2 with structurally different proofs.
- **Lower bound** (\(\Omega(\sigma T)\) on any causal eviction policy): failed in Round 3.

**Neither direction produced a verifiable proof at the headline rate the paper claims.** The proofs are not salvageable by polish; they require structural narrowing of the coverage class (upper bound) or substantive re-derivation of the per-block calculation plus replacement of the trivial tightness baseline (lower bound). Both of those are multi-month efforts, not days-of-revision efforts.

**Recommendation: commit to MobiSys 2028 systems track and defer theory to ICLR 2028 or NeurIPS 2027 with a fresh theoretical framing.** The systems contribution (OnePlus 15 measurement infrastructure, V1FA2 policy stack, per-step \(\epsilon_t\) instrumentation, thermal/throttle characterization) stands on its own and does not require either of the failed theorems. Attempting to push either Theorem 1 (upper) or the cumulative-regret lower bound to a 2026/2027 deadline carries acceptance probability well below the threshold the project committed to at kickoff.

Concretely, the next 6 months should be allocated as follows:

1. **MobiSys 2028 systems track.** Lock the empirical story (V1FA2 stack, per-step \(\epsilon_t\), thermal, throttle, energy). Theorem 1 is demoted to an informal motivation lemma with no `\begin{theorem}` block. No formal regret proof in the MobiSys submission.
2. **Theory-side reset.** Step back from both "upper bound on a specific policy" and "lower bound on all policies." A fresh framing — for example, a conditional bound parameterized by a measured per-head sparsity exponent, or a separation theorem between policy classes rather than an absolute rate — is more likely to survive adversarial verification than another attempt at either of the two failed directions.
3. **Instrumentation first, theorem second.** Per-head \(\gamma\) histograms and per-step \(\epsilon_t\) measurements from real OnePlus 15 traces should land before any new theorem is drafted. The Round-1/Round-2 failures both stemmed from assumptions that were not empirically grounded; the Round-3 failure stemmed from a construction that does not resemble real attention streams. Real data first prevents the same failure mode on Round 4.
