# Thermal Co-Management for Sustained On-Device LLM Inference

## 1. Problem framing
On the OnePlus 15 (Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5), Phi-3-mini long-decode under the v1_FA2 selective baseline reaches a peak DDR temperature of 72.9 C and a peak CPU temperature of 78.4 C, with the Qualcomm kernel mitigation framework forcing a transient downshift to 883 MHz once the DDR sensor crosses approximately 65 C. The kernel's step-wise governor is reactive: it observes a trip crossing, requests a cooling-device transition through freq_qos, waits a 100-300 ms zone-poll period, then re-evaluates. In a memory-bound autoregressive decode that streams KV and weight tiles every token, this lag is enough to produce visible throughput cliffs (the iter-10 event) even though mean throughput - 6.75 tok/s - remains acceptable. Because LPDDR5X self-heating is bandwidth-dominated rather than CPU-V^2-f-dominated, the binding thermal constraint is not the CPU clock the baseline already pins (1632/1497 MHz), but the memory subsystem's sustained traffic. Reducing peak DDR temperature without losing mean throughput is therefore the central design problem.

## 2. Survey of the five mitigation families
**CPU DVFS.** Techniques in this family (UCLAMP, schedutil up_rate_limit, EAS toggles, cooling-device cur_state, OPP table pruning, no_turbo, schedtune boost) are largely shadowed by the existing 1632/1497 MHz pin: when the freq ceiling is already low, secondary scheduler-side caps are no-ops or fight each other. Their value is as backstops, not primary levers.

**Memory bandwidth.** This is the family that directly attacks the binding constraint. Devfreq governor pinning, LLCC/DDR icc vote reduction, BWMON re-tuning, and devfreq_cooling cur_state override each promise 2-6 C of DDR headroom but at variable throughput cost (-6% to -30%). The Pareto-best operating point is a moderate ~75% bus-frequency cap rather than a hard floor to the lowest OPP.

**Workload distribution.** sched_setaffinity to perf cores, cpuset partitioning, core_ctl cluster sizing, and prime-vs-perf heat-spreader routing redistribute heat within the SoC. Because DDR sits in a package-on-package stack above the SoC and is heated mostly by memory-controller activity rather than which logical core issued the load, these techniques yield modest DDR effects (0.5-2 C) on this workload.

**Algorithmic.** KV-cache quantization (Q8_0, Q4_0, KIVI), structural sparsity (Quest, SqueezeAttention, H2O, KVzip), and StreamingLLM-style sliding windows reduce bytes per token at the cost of small PPL drift. INT8 KV is uniquely attractive because Phi-3 uses grouped-query attention and the GQA layout already shrinks raw KV; quantizing what remains gives a clean ~2x bytes/token reduction with PPL drift inside measurement noise. Aggressive sparsity (Quest, KIVI INT2) promises much more but requires multi-week kernel work on mobile NEON/HVX targets.

**Hardware offload.** Hexagon HTP/HVX, Adreno OpenCL, and PowerInfer-2 style heterogeneous CPU+NPU pipelines can move 4-6 C off the DDR sensor in principle, but on this workload they either (a) shift heat to a different shared rail without reducing total LPDDR5X traffic, (b) require multi-week QNN graph integration that conflicts with the v1_FA2 selective code path, or (c) fail the exact-fidelity requirement of the Phi-3 repro by forcing INT4 weight quantization for HMX graph-prepare.

## 3. Why the Q8 + bus-cap + closed-loop-K trio
The selected stack chooses the lowest-risk member of each productive family. INT8 KV quantization is a hygiene-tier change with monotone benefit on a GQA model [Lin et al., KIVI, arXiv:2402.02750; Hooper et al., KVQuant, arXiv:2401.18079]. Devfreq bus capping at ~75% of peak directly attacks DDR self-heating with deterministic effect on PPL [the same lever explored in MNN-AECS and in Kim et al., arXiv:2507.02135]. The closed-loop K controller is the dissertation's Track-2 contribution: it operationalizes temperature as a first-class scheduling signal, modulating an existing v1_FA2 selective parameter rather than introducing a new attention algorithm. The pattern descends directly from H2O [Zhang et al., arXiv:2306.14048] and StreamingLLM [Xiao et al., arXiv:2309.17453], but inverts their fixed-budget assumption: where prior work picks a static K, we let temperature pick K dynamically with hysteresis.

## 4. Composability and the Track-2 closed-loop angle
The three levers are orthogonal in mechanism (bytes per token, bandwidth ceiling, retained-token count) and therefore stack sub-additively but in the same direction. Q8 first reshapes the bandwidth demand the bus cap will see; the bus cap then sets a thermal contract the K controller can plan around; the controller spends its small PPL budget only when the contract is at risk of breach. This is the canonical closed-loop pattern from the GreenLLM/zTT line of work - sense the thermal zone, act on a runtime knob, dwell with hysteresis - with the knob chosen to lie inside the existing FA2 kernel rather than in cpufreq sysfs. That keeps the controller off the Qualcomm thermal HAL's contested write path.

## 5. Limits and honest caveats
The race-to-idle versus pace-the-load tension is unresolved: race-to-idle is the right strategy for short bursts but counter-productive for sustained memory-bound decode, where the kernel's own throttle re-fires earlier under racing. We therefore exclude race-to-idle. Techniques requiring retraining (Medusa, LayerSkip, CLA/YOCO, LoLCATs linearization) are excluded under the exact-fidelity constraint of the PowerInfer-2 repro. The closed-loop K controller's expected gain is moderate-confidence at best - the +0.08 PPL ceiling assumes Phi-3 tolerates K=256 in its hot state, which we have not yet measured on long-context retrieval. Bus-cap effectiveness is gated on Qualcomm RPMh not overriding the userspace vote, which we verify with a re-read asserter loop but cannot guarantee on retail firmware.
