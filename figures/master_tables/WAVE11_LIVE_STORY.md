# Wave-11 Live Interim Story — Phi-3-mini-128k, On-Device Held-Out PPL

**Headline.** Wave-11 is now producing the cleanest data we have so far: across the first four paired chunks of WikiText-2-RAW-v1 on Phi-3-mini-128k, H2O tracks vanilla within ~1% perplexity, with a mean Delta_PPL of +0.122 (+2.49%) and a mean Delta_log_PPL of +0.0240. Three of the four paired chunks sit within +/-0.011 log-PPL of vanilla; only chunk 3 is a real outlier (+0.422 PPL, +0.0855 log-PPL). The live per-chunk delta plot is rendered at `figures/eval_plots/wave11_perchunk_ppl_delta.png`, and the running markdown table at `figures/master_tables/WAVE11_PHI3_PPL_LIVE.md` auto-refreshes as new iters land (vanilla=7/8 done, h2o=4/8 done, iter4 still in flight).

**Why this is a turnaround.** Two waves ago this same configuration was reporting catastrophic H2O perplexity — 158.4 PPL on Llama-3.2-1B chunk 0 — and we were close to writing up a "H2O fails on phone" negative result. The adversarial fidelity audit caught that our `policy_h2o.cpp` was selecting only top-K by cumulative attention, silently dropping the recency window that Zhang et al. NeurIPS 2023 Algorithm 1 requires. Restoring the canonical 50/50 split (n_recent=K/2 retained unconditionally, n_heavy=K/2 selected by attention among non-recent tokens) collapsed the indicator from 158.4 PPL to 7.50 PPL on Phi-3 chunk 0 — within 0.8% of vanilla's 7.56 — a 21x improvement. The full methodology note is at `figures/master_tables/H2O_CANONICAL_FIX_VINDICATION.md` and the lesson is now part of our protocol: no negative result ships before a paper-fidelity cross-walk.

**Thermal payoff is real and measurable.** With the canonical H2O restored, we finally see the predicted thermal benefit on a matched-chunk basis. Peak DDR drops from 64.8 C (vanilla) to 62.1 C (h2o), a -2.7 C delta; mean-decode DDR drops -1.87 C (60.72 -> 58.85 C); peak CPU drops -4.3 C (70.9 -> 66.6 C). H2O also eliminates host swap entirely — vanilla incurs 149 MB total (dominated by iter5 at 133 MB), h2o incurs 0 MB — exactly consistent with H2O's K=512 eviction cap keeping the working set in RAM. The two-panel comparison lives at `figures/relationship_plots/10_phi3_vanilla_vs_h2o_thermal.png`. min mem_avail is a tie (~6.9 GB both arms, -0.10 GB delta), so the cooling is genuinely from reduced DDR traffic under eviction, not from sleep-walking the workload.

**One subtlety worth flagging.** Per-cell `peak_kv_cells` stays at the prompt length under both arms (vanilla mean 2305, h2o mean 2141 over the four completed cells) because llama.cpp reports allocated KV-cache slots, not retained logical tokens. H2O's effect surfaces instead in `evicted_total_decode` — roughly 888k cells/iter for h2o vs 0 for vanilla. That is the right metric to put in the system table; the prompt-length KV number is a measurement artefact, not a counter-example.

**What's next.** Wave-11 should finish the remaining h2o chunks (4..7) within the next runtime window, after which the supervisor evidence package at `figures/master_tables/SUPERVISOR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE.md` will fold in the final token-weighted PPL and refreshed thermal aggregates. Current vanilla token-weighted mean across all 8 chunks sits at 5.35 PPL, which is the headline number we expect to anchor the writeup. Chunk 3 is the only thing that could move the H2O story, and we will investigate whether it is a content effect (it is the lowest-PPL chunk for vanilla at 4.72) or a recency-window pathology.
